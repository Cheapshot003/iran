01Who We Are

The Veresk Institute is a bridge institution at the interface between Europe and Iran. Its seat is in Vienna; a parallel seat in Tehran is planned as soon as political conditions allow.

Our name refers to the Veresk Bridge in the Alborz Mountains — inaugurated in 1936 under Reza Shah Pahlavi and built by a multinational team from Sweden, Germany, Austria and Italy. Its chief engineer, the Austrian Walter Aigner, chose to be buried at Veresk.

The bridge stands for what we want to make possible again: cooperation between Iran and Europe as equals, built on shared achievement rather than mistrust, on construction rather than dependency.

02What We Reject

We expressly distance ourselves from the appeasement tradition that has shaped European engagement with Iran for decades. Agreements such as the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) were deals with a regime rather than with a nation. They stabilised the Islamic Republic economically without ending its regional aggression or its domestic repression, and they excluded the Iranian population from the negotiating tables.

We reject dealings that grant an unjust regime legitimacy and resources. The system of the Islamic Republic is not reformable in its substance; the notion that change could succeed from within the apparatus has proven to be an illusion. We therefore place no hope in "reformers" inside the regime, but in the forces working toward a democratic transition after the end of the Islamic Republic.

03Our Reference

Until the definitive fall of the Islamic Republic, the Veresk Institute recognizes the Iran Prosperity Project and the transition plan developed under the leadership of Reza Pahlavi and his team as its authoritative frame of reference, and actively supports this endeavour.

We share its analysis that, after the end of the Islamic Republic, an orderly transitional period will be necessary to secure economic stability, rebuild institutions and pave the way to a democratically elected government.

We take no decision on Iran's future form of government. As foreseen in the transition plan, this is a question the Iranian people will answer themselves in a free referendum. We commit to the inviolable principles that carry this transition:

Secularism and the rule of law

Individual liberties and democracy

Separation of powers and judicial independence

The territorial integrity of Iran

— A policy with the people In place of a policy with the dictatorship, we put a policy with the Iranian people.

04What We Do

The Veresk Institute prepares, today, the European-Iranian bridges that a free Iran will need from its first day — so that when transition comes, cooperation does not have to be improvised, but can begin on prepared ground.

  • Politics & Administration Briefings and policy recommendations for parliamentarians, ministries, embassies and European institutions, preparing the diplomatic and legal groundwork for rapid normalisation.
  • Business Advising companies, industry associations and investors preparing for an opening Iranian market, and connecting them with Iranian professionals and exile experts in key reconstruction sectors.
  • Citizens & Civil Society A point of contact for European citizens interested in Iran and for Iranians interested in Europe — laying the groundwork for genuine people-to-people ties.
  • Science & Education Connecting European universities and research institutions with Iranian academics in exile; preparing higher-education partnerships for the day of transition.
  • Media & the Public A point of contact for journalists, conveying Iranian discourses into the German-speaking sphere, and contributing to fact-based public debate on Iran.
05Our Self-Understanding

The Veresk Institute is non-partisan in European domestic politics, but not without opinion on the Iran question. We take a stand for democracy, the rule of law, human rights and the territorial integrity of Iran. We work in a fact-based and transparent manner and in close coordination with the voices of the democratic transition.

Our conviction: a free, prosperous Iran will not only restore dignity and prosperity to the Iranian people, but will also be a stabilising force in the Middle East and a natural partner for Europe.

The Veresk Bridge once proved that Europeans and Iranians can build the impossible together.

We intend to bring these aspirations back to life.
